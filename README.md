---

## 🧩 Overview

Basic Tenets of Psychoanalysis brings lectures, discussion, and oral assessment into one course room. The professor teaches aloud through ElevenLabs speech. Students can listen at their preferred speed, ask questions by speaking or writing, and hear the professor respond.

The course combines five lectures with post-lecture assessments and a final oral examination. Its central demand is judgment: consider the evidence, distinguish competing explanations, and defend an interpretation.

This is **not a text chat with optional audio**. Speech is part of the teaching experience.

---

## 👥 Who It’s For

- **Students of psychology and psychoanalysis** who want an accessible introduction with room for questions.
- **Independent learners** who prefer listening and conversation to reading alone.
- **Readers of Freud and the psychoanalytic tradition** who want to explore concepts through concrete situations.
- **Teachers and discussion leaders** looking for examples of application-based oral learning.
- **Curious thinkers** who want to challenge an interpretation rather than simply repeat it.

---

## ⚙️ Core Capabilities

- **Spoken lectures** — Professor speech is generated by ElevenLabs and played aloud as part of the course.
- **Adjustable listening speed** — Change the pace of the lecture without changing its content.
- **Male and female professor voices** — Choose the lecture voice you prefer.
- **Four conversational avatars** — Talk with Dr. Male, Dr. Female, Freud, or Kernberg from the professor controls at the top of the screen.
- **Two-way spoken discussion** — Click the microphone once to turn it on. Click again to turn it off and send your question. No pressing and holding.
- **Written questions, spoken replies** — Type instead of speaking when that is more convenient; the professor still answers aloud.
- **Course-informed discussion** — Ask for clarification, raise objections, propose alternatives, and explore speculation. The professors distinguish hypotheses from established facts.
- **Ungraded access to the professor** — Discussion is separate from assessment and does not count toward points or credit.
- **Applied assessment questions** — Each question presents a fresh situation and asks the student to adjudicate it.
- **Recorded-answer playback** — Listen to your own recorded assessment answer.
- **Direct assessment access** — Open post-lecture assessments and the final oral exam through dedicated links.
- **Progress and results** — Review course progress and recorded assessment results.
- **Restart the class** — Begin a fresh course run without deleting earlier work or changing another browser’s progress.

## 💡 Understanding, Not Memorization

The assessment should not ask students to recall what made a particular character jealous in a story they have already heard.

Instead, it presents a **new situation** and asks for a reasoned judgment.

> **Illustrative question:** A manager dismisses reasonable criticism as jealousy, yet becomes intensely anxious before every performance review. What interpretations fit these observations? What alternative explanation should be considered, and what additional evidence would change your judgment?

The task is not to guess a hidden answer. It is to explain what the evidence supports, where uncertainty remains, and why one interpretation may be stronger than another.

---

## 🚀 What Makes It Different

- **The professor speaks.** Teaching and replies are delivered aloud, not left as silent text.
- **Conversation belongs inside the course.** Students can question and explore without turning every exchange into a graded task.
- **Cases require judgment.** Familiar concepts are applied to unfamiliar situations.
- **Different perspectives are available.** General professors sit alongside Freud and Kernberg AI portrayals.
- **The microphone is a toggle.** Speaking does not require holding a button down.
- **Listening is adjustable.** Students control lecture speed and can review their own recorded answers.
- **The course is currently open-access.** There is no sign-in wall in the current version.

## 🧭 Getting Started as a Student

1. Open the course room and select a lecture.
2. Choose a lecture voice and adjust the listening speed.
3. Select a professor avatar whenever you want a discussion.
4. Use **Speak** for a microphone conversation or **Write** to type your question.
5. Open a **Post-lecture assessment** or the **Final oral exam** when you want to practice applying the material.
6. Use **Restart class** when you want a fresh course run.

For spoken input, allow microphone access when your browser requests it and keep your speakers or headphones available for replies.

### 🔐 Configuration

The running app uses server-side credentials for OpenAI, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs, and the Genius 101 Freud and Kernberg integrations:

`OPENAI_API_KEY` · `ASSEMBLYAI_API_KEY` · `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` · `FREUD_API_KEY` · `KERNBERG_API_KEY`

Database and browser-progress configuration also require `DATABASE_URL` and `SESSION_SECRET`. The web service requires its configured `PORT` and `BASE_PATH`; the API server requires its own `PORT`.

**Keep credentials out of the README, browser code, screenshots, and source control.** ElevenLabs speech uses the creator’s own securely stored API key.
